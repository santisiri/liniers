## ep01-sc01 — THE RUMOUR OF SAILS (INT. FONDA — DAY)

**Fuente** [04:52–05:13]: "Hacia 1806… esta era una zona relativamente marginal, donde se suele decir que había una siesta colonial — es una caricatura con la que se la suele contar—. Hasta junio de 1806. […] Sobremonte […] empieza a recibir noticias de que hay barcos de guerra ingleses merodeando la zona." La ciudad no cree en la guerra; la fuente relativiza la "siesta": acá la ciudad trabaja, no duerme — simplemente no imagina que alguien la quiera.

**Action**: Title card over black: **BUENOS AIRES, CAPITAL OF THE VICEROYALTY OF THE RÍO DE LA PLATA. JUNE 1806.** A winter noon, silver-grey light. The fonda — the eating-house near the fort — in full swing: braziers, steam off the puchero stew, tables of merchants, porters, a pair of friars. MARCELA (22, reddened hands, a half-done braid) comes and goes with her tray. A FISHERMAN enters, soaked through, and the news falls out of his mouth before the cold does.

**Dialogue**:

**FISHERMAN:** Sails. Off the mouth of the river. Big ones — men-of-war.

**PATRON 1** *(without setting down his fork)*: Men-of-war… Smugglers, more like. Same as ever.

**FISHERMAN:** A whole fleet, smuggling?

**PATRON 2** *(laughter)*: Then cloth is about to get cheaper. Sit down, man — it's going cold on you.

*(Laughter round the tables. The fisherman sits, defeated. Marcela sets a plate before him. She is not laughing.)*

**MARCELA:** How many sails, did you say?

**FISHERMAN:** Many, child. Many.

**MARCELA** *(gathering plates, drying her hands on her apron)*: A lot of sail, just to come and sell.

*(No one listens to her. Through the window, far off, the leaden river. Marcela looks at it for a second — barely a second — and goes on working.)*

**Visual note**: The pot's steam fogging the window; behind the dirty glass, the endless river with the tips of sails barely suggested in the mist. The one still face in the frame is Marcela's: the camera finds her as it will always find the people — in the foreground, working.

## ep01-sc02 — THE MADMAN AND THE PRIZE (EXT. DECK OF THE FLAGSHIP, RÍO DE LA PLATA — DUSK)

**Fuente** [02:27–04:52]: Trafalgar (1805) deja a Inglaterra "dueña de los mares del mundo" y a España "prácticamente sin barcos"; "gran parte del tesoro de estas colonias empieza a acumularse en el Río de la Plata". [07:13]: 1.600 hombres, tropa muy experimentada, los Highlanders del 71. [10:40–12:45]: Popham, "el loco", arquitecto de la invasión, viene **sin autorización oficial** — "se manda como un pirata" desde el Cabo de Buena Esperanza; "dudaba de si tenía que garantizar la independencia o no"; su forma de convencer era el botín; su locura, expresión de "un imperio con una visión impresionante".

**Action**: The fleet at anchor on the brown river, sails furled, an ashen dusk light. On deck, soldiers of the 71st whet bayonets; a piper tries two notes. POPHAM (43, navy blue, a cocksure stance: weight on one leg, elbow on the gunwale) sweeps the flat city with his spyglass. BERESFORD (38, heavyset, a clouded left eye) motionless at his side. Off to one side, GILLESPIE (36, spare, a marine's coat) writes in a notebook propped on the rail.

**Dialogue** *(English, subtitled)*:

**POPHAM** *(without lowering the spyglass)*: Look at it, General. Flat as a table. Rich as an altar. The silver of half a continent piles up in that river — and since Trafalgar, Spain hasn't a ship left to carry it home.

**BERESFORD:** We have no orders, Commodore.

**POPHAM:** We have sixteen hundred of the best troops in the world and a city fast asleep. Orders would only slow us down.

**BERESFORD:** And when London asks what we were doing here?

**POPHAM** *(lowers the spyglass; smiles)*: London forgives everything the day the treasure sails in. Everything.

**BERESFORD** *(pause; looks at the shore)*: And these people. Are we setting them free, or taking their city?

**POPHAM:** We'll decide once we're inside. *(to Gillespie, without looking at him)* Write that down, Captain: the river offered no resistance.

**GILLESPIE** *(writes; without looking up)*: The river never does, sir. Cities are another matter.

**Visual note**: Popham's spyglass frames a low city of colonial towers and kitchen smoke — defenceless as a laid table. His navy blue is the only blue in the frame against the redcoat red of the deck: the man of the sea who will never quite set foot on land.

## ep01-sc03 — SMUGGLERS (INT. THE FORT, THE VICEROY'S STUDY — DAY)

**Fuente** [04:52–05:39]: "¿Quién gobierna? El virrey Sobremonte, que a comienzos, mediados de junio, empieza a recibir noticias de que hay barcos de guerra ingleses merodeando la zona. Y el tipo dice: 'No pasa nada, deben ser contrabandistas, como estamos acostumbrados. Si van a ir, van a ir a Montevideo.'"

**Action**: An overloaded study: velvet, royal portraits, a silver brazier. SOBREMONTE (61, bewigged, everyday court dress) signs documents with the ceremony of a mass. A NAVAL OFFICER, boots caked in mud, waits out the end of each flourish. At the back, chests bearing the royal seal.

**Dialogue**:

**NAVAL OFFICER:** Excellency: war sails off the coast. The pilots can find no end to them.

**SOBREMONTE** *(without looking up; the gesture of waving away a fly)*: Smugglers. As we are accustomed. This river knows nothing else.

**NAVAL OFFICER:** With respect, Excellency: that is a great deal of sail for contraband.

**SOBREMONTE** *(blots the ink, blows on it)*: If they come for anything, they will go to Montevideo. Montevideo has walls. What could there be here that an Englishman might want?

*(The officer looks at the chests bearing the royal seal. He says nothing.)*

**NAVAL OFFICER:** Shall I call out the militias, Excellency?

**SOBREMONTE:** Let us not alarm the city over a handful of gulls. *(rises, adjusts his sash)* Besides — tonight His Excellency attends the theatre.

**Visual note**: The final shot widens: the small viceroy among enormous painted kings, and in the foreground the chests of the treasure — the answer to his own question, gleaming at his back.

## ep01-sc04 — THE INTERRUPTED PERFORMANCE (INT. THEATRE — NIGHT)

**Fuente** [05:39–06:25]: "El 27 de junio está Sobremonte mirando una obra de teatro […] de humor bastante tontuelo. Y en el medio de la obra tienen que interrumpir al virrey para decirle: —Virrey, le quiero decir una cosa. Los contrabandistas están llegando. —Virrey: están adentro. Están adentro, Sobremonte." (La obra no se nombra en pantalla — fact-check #5. La fuente comprime teatro y toma de la ciudad en el 27 [05:39, 06:25→07:13]; en pantalla el teatro queda como **la víspera, sin fecha** — criterio del fact-check #8: no anclar cartel donde la fuente comprime — y el cartel del 27 va al amanecer de la caída, en sc05.)

**Action**: The eve of the fall. A colonial theatre by candlelight: the élite in the stalls, the common people up above in the cazuela — the upper gallery. On stage, a silly little comedy: a lady feigns fainting fits, the leading man trips; laughter. In the centre box, SOBREMONTE laughs with his mouth shut, applauds with two fingers. The AIDE-DE-CAMP enters, pale, and bends to his ear. The play goes on below while the whisper climbs.

**Dialogue**:

**AIDE-DE-CAMP** *(a whisper)*: Excellency… the smugglers.

**SOBREMONTE** *(eyes still on the stage)*: What of the smugglers?

**AIDE-DE-CAMP:** They are landing. They are troops, Excellency. They are an army.

*(Sobremonte turns his head slowly. On stage, the actress speaks her verse; no one in the box is laughing now.)*

**SOBREMONTE:** Where?

**AIDE-DE-CAMP:** Excellency… they are inside. They are inside the city.

*(Silence. Sobremonte looks about him — decorum first: who is watching. Half the stalls have already turned toward the box. He rises, straightens his coat, salutes the house with the smallest of bows, as if withdrawing to supper.)*

**SOBREMONTE** *(to the aide-de-camp, leaving, his voice cracking beneath the protocol)*: Have them harness the coach. No running. *(beat)* No running, I said.

*(Below, the comedy carries on for one more verse, alone, in the silence of the whole house staring at the empty box.)*

**Visual note**: The box candle trembles with the slammed door; the viceroy backlit as he leaves without running — running with his shoulders. Up in the cazuela, the faces of the people know it before the stalls do: the first time the episode looks down at power from above.

## ep01-sc05 — LITTLE FIRES (EXT. FORT COURTYARD / STREETS — NIGHT AND DAWN)

**Fuente** [05:39–07:59]: Sobremonte "sale a armar, con lo que tiene a mano —que es muy poco—, desorganizado, improvisado, algún tipo de resistencia"; Belgrano "rápido quiere ir a defender, y lo que se encuentra es una desorganización total […] Enojadísimo"; "la resistencia […] fueron unos fueguitos que tiramos y que los ingleses se sacaron de encima rápidamente"; "'Sentí vergüenza, sentí indignación de no saber nada de las milicias y no poder combatirlos'"; "El 27 entran los ingleses, toman la ciudad sin combatir, prácticamente sin combatir." (El cartel del 27 va acá, sobre el amanecer de la caída — el único día que la fuente ancla con firmeza; ver nota de sc04.)

**Action**: Night — the same night as the theatre. The fort courtyard: torches, chaos. Old militiamen roll a cannon with no carriage; rusted muskets are handed out from a chest; no one forms up, no one commands. BELGRANO (36, a functionary's frock coat, crooked cravat) arrives at a run. He asks for a weapon; they give him a musket. His calligrapher's fingers fight the ramrod; he drops it; an OLD MILITIAMAN hands it back without mockery — he doesn't know how either. Far off, scattered shots: little fires flaring and dying in the small hours. Grey dawn — brief title card: **27 JUNE.** — the red columns advance unhurried through the mud; the defenders dissolve into doorways. Belgrano, against a recova arcade, his musket unfired, watches his surrendered city go by.

**Dialogue**:

**BELGRANO** *(in the courtyard, to the chaos)*: Who commands here? Who holds the command?

**OLD MILITIAMAN** *(handing him the ramrod)*: Well… you, doctor, if you care to.

*(Belgrano looks at him. That is the whole of the army: one old man, a chest of rusted muskets, a cannon without wheels.)*

**BELGRANO** *(at dawn, to an OFFICIAL sheltering beside him; voice low, fist clenched behind his back)*: Forty-five thousand souls… and not ten who know how to load a musket. Nor do I. *(pause)* Shame is what I feel. Shame, and indignation — at knowing nothing of the militia. At not being able to fight them.

**OFFICIAL:** It's over, Don Manuel. It's done.

**BELGRANO** *(watching the red column)*: No. It is not over. *(beat)* To be free, we shall have to learn to defend ourselves. Beginning with me.

**Visual note**: The little fires: isolated musket flashes in the night, going out one by one like blown candles. At first light, the wide shot: a thin red column entering an immense grey city that does not shoot.

## ep01-sc06 — SIXTEEN HUNDRED (EXT. MAIN STREET TOWARD THE PLAZA — DAY)

**Fuente** [07:13–07:59]: "1.600 hombres en una ciudad de —hay discusión sobre los números— 45.000 habitantes. […] Es una tropa muy experimentada: con los Highlanders, el regimiento número 71 […] venían de Escocia, pelearon en la independencia norteamericana, pelearon contra el ejército napoleónico."

**Action**: The 71st Highlanders enter at the march up the muddy street: kilts, pipes, fixed bayonets, the veteran's look on every face. The people watch in silence: the NEIGHBOUR WOMAN (52, in half-mourning) from her rooftop, with vertical contempt; the PORTER (30), empty hands clenched; the SLAVE (35), head down, registering everything; the PULPERO — keeper of the pulpería, the tavern-store — in his doorway, dropping his voice an octave; the saddler FATHER (40) with the BOY (8) by the hand. In the doorway of the fonda, MARCELA dries her hands on her apron and does not lower her eyes. A British OFFICER shouts an order in English to clear the street: no one understands, no one moves; a soldier shoves with his musket butt and the row of neighbours falls back one step — one, no more.

**Dialogue**:

**BOY** *(tugging at his father's hand)*: Papa… are those men wearing skirts?

**FATHER:** Hush now.

**BOY:** Then why does nobody chase them off?

**FATHER** *(eyes never leaving the column; a large truth in a small voice)*: Because they caught us sleeping, son.

**BRITISH OFFICER** *(to the row of neighbours)*: Make way! Clear the street!

*(No one moves. It is not defiance: it is that no one understands. The officer repeats it louder, as if volume were translation.)*

**NEIGHBOUR WOMAN** *(from her rooftop, to herself, spitting the words)*: Shout away, shout away. Down here not even the dog understands you.

**Visual note**: A Fouqueray composition inverted: the still crowd fills the frame edge to edge and the red column is barely a line crossing it — 1,600 men swallowed by 45,000 who do not yet know what they are. The pipes sound like victory; the close shots of clenched hands sound like something else.

## ep01-sc07 — FIVE HOURS LATER (INT. FONDA — NIGHT)

**Fuente** [13:05–13:32]: la humillación "va a empezar a aparecer muy rápido, apenas cinco horas después de la invasión. Gillespie […] va con sus compañeros a una fonda, a un restaurante cerca del fuerte, a comer." (La escena se recrea como ficción propia — dec-003; el clip de 1952 no se usa.)

**Action**: The night of the 27th — the city fell with the dawn; five hours after the entry, the invader is already at supper. The fonda at half-light, half empty; the patrons who remain eat staring at their plates. GILLESPIE and TWO British OFFICERS come in: the silence thickens until it could be cut. They choose a table. Marcela serves them without looking at them, her tray held like a shield. Gillespie observes it all — the faces, the backs, the rancour seated at the other tables — and opens his notebook beside his plate.

**Dialogue**:

**OFFICER 1** *(English)*: Five hours, and not a single shot fired in anger. I've had rougher landings at home.

**GILLESPIE** *(English, looking at the tables)*: Look at their faces, Lieutenant. This isn't over. It hasn't begun.

**PATRON 1** *(under his breath, into his wine)*: At my table. They eat at my table, the heretics.

**OFFICER 2** *(in chewed Spanish, to Marcela)*: What… is there… to eat?

**MARCELA** *(not looking at him)*: What's left.

**GILLESPIE** *(in broken Spanish, with genuine curiosity)*: And what is… "what's left"?

**MARCELA** *(clearing a glass from the next table)*: Not much.

*(The officers laugh; Gillespie does not. He follows her with his eyes, wets his pencil, writes.)*

**OFFICER 1** *(English)*: What in God's name are you writing now?

**GILLESPIE** *(English)*: Everything. Somebody should.

**Visual note**: The redcoat red as an intruding stain on the fonda's mud-and-silver palette. In the foreground, the open notebook beside the steaming plate; at the back, out of focus, the criollo backs like a wall. Marcela understands scraps of the English — it shows in a blink, never on her face.

## ep01-sc08 — THE VICEROY'S OX-CARTS (EXT. PAMPA ROAD — GREY DAY)

**Fuente** [09:35–10:23]: "el colmo de los colmos es que la autoridad política que debería defender esta invasión —el virrey Sobremonte— huye a Córdoba con el tesoro. […] supuestamente se va a resguardar el tesoro […] y supuestamente se va a armar tropas para efectivamente resistir. Pasa que lo hace con una lentitud desesperante." (El "colmo de los colmos" [09:35] es orden retórico de la narración, no cronología: la fuente no fecha la partida, y su propia lógica — "resguardar el tesoro **de las nuevas autoridades**" [10:23] — la ancla antes de la incautación británica; el andamiaje de la biblia ubica el abandono en torno a la caída del 27/6. El destino final del tesoro — "termina yendo a Gran Bretaña" [10:23] — no se dramatiza acá: la cita está dentro del rango de ep01, pero el hecho ocurre en la cronología del tramo de ep03 [35:05].)

**Action**: The endless pampa, a leaden sky. Days on the road: the short caravan that left the city the night of the theatre — ox-carts loaded with chests bearing the royal seal, badly covered with tarpaulins; a scant escort. SOBREMONTE in a travelling cloak, his wig stored in its box upon his knees, his own hair thin and grey: a viceroy half-undressed of his authority. The AIDE-DE-CAMP — the same one from the theatre box — rides beside the cart. A wheel sinks into the mud; the peons wait for the order; the order is long in coming.

**Dialogue**:

**AIDE-DE-CAMP:** Excellency: if we lighten the carts, we reach Córdoba in half the time.

**SOBREMONTE:** Lighten? This is the King's silver. The King's silver is not lightened: it is safeguarded.

**AIDE-DE-CAMP:** And Buenos Aires, Excellency? In the meantime?

**SOBREMONTE** *(the fly-waving gesture, slower than ever)*: Buenos Aires… will resist as best it can. In Córdoba I shall raise an army worthy of the name, and we shall return with order. *(beat; settles his travelling cloak)* We left the theatre without running, my good sir: we shall not run now through the mud. *(beat)* And write this down, let it stand in the dispatch: the viceroy does not flee. The viceroy withdraws. With the treasure.

**AIDE-DE-CAMP** *(pause; writes)*: …Withdraws. With the treasure.

**Visual note**: The widest shot of the episode: the tiny caravan on the empty pampa, advancing at oxen's pace — power departing at the exact speed of its own uselessness. Under the ill-tied tarpaulin, the gleam of the royal seal: the only wealth in the scene travels with its back to its city.

## ep01-sc09 — A CONQUEST IN DUE FORM (INT. THE FORT, HALL OF GOVERNMENT / EXT. THE PLAZA RECOVA — DAY / EXT. THE FORT — DAWN THE FOLLOWING DAY)

**Fuente** [07:59–09:12]: "Beresford se proclama gobernador de estas tierras. Se incautan los bienes públicos. […] Mandan un barco a Inglaterra a avisar que tienen nuevas conquistas y a pedir refuerzos […] confirman, ratifican las instituciones españolas, a los funcionarios españoles, solo que ahora un peldaño abajo. […] 'Es la graciable intención de Su Majestad británica abrir un comercio libre y permitido, semejante al de las otras colonias inglesas.' […] Al día siguiente se iza la bandera británica en el fuerte."

**Action**: The fort's hall of government, now with redcoat sentries. BERESFORD signs without looking up; soldiers carry out the counting-house ledgers and files and seal the storeroom doors with wax (the confiscation of the public property). At the back, standing open, the strong room: empty shelves, the marks in the dust where the chests with the royal seal once stood. A line of Spanish OFFICIALS waits, hats in hand. POPHAM by the window, watching the harbour. — Cut to the Recova arcade: a criollo TOWN CRIER reads the proclamation in Spanish to the mute crowd. — Ellipsis: the following day, at dawn, the British flag rises over the fort.

**Dialogue**:

**BERESFORD** *(English, to the SENIOR OFFICIAL, through a halting interpreter)*: You keep your office, señor. Same desk. Same seal. One step down.

**SENIOR OFFICIAL** *(swallows; bows)*: …Understood, Excellency.

**POPHAM** *(English, at the window, without turning round)*: The strong room, General. Empty. Ledgers, seals, clerks — everything but the silver. Your viceroy rode out with the treasure before we reached the Plaza. In ox carts, they say.

**BERESFORD** *(still signing)*: Then it travels at the speed of oxen. First the city, Commodore. The silver will keep.

**POPHAM** *(English, from the window)*: The ship sails for London within the hour. The news, and the request for reinforcements. Two months there and back — three, with bad winds.

**BERESFORD** *(still signing)*: Then until it returns, Commodore, this city must believe the matter is settled.

**POPHAM:** It is settled. Look outside: not a stone thrown.

*(Beresford looks up for the first time. The clouded eye, fixed on Popham, says nothing.)*

— EXT. THE RECOVA:

**TOWN CRIER** *(in Spanish, his voice bouncing off the silence)*: "It is the gracious intention of His Britannic Majesty to open a free and permitted trade, similar to that of the other English colonies…!"

*(Faces of stone. The neighbour woman slams her shutters. The pulpero listens from his doorway, rag over his shoulder. The boy, playing among the legs of the crowd, parrots the singsong without understanding it:)*

**BOY:** Grayshus intenshun…! Grayshus intenshun…!

*(The father catches him by one arm, without violence, and carries him off.)*

— EXT. THE FORT — DAWN THE FOLLOWING DAY:

*(No crier and no public: the British flag climbs slowly up the fort's flagpole, heavy with drizzle. Below, the plaza nearly empty; the PORTER, crossing with his load on his shoulder, stops for one step, watches it rise, and goes on.)*

**Visual note**: Beresford's pen and the red wax over Spanish stamped paper: conquest as paperwork. In the plaza, a proclamation conceived in English cried out in Spanish — the first time the city hears its new master, and it hears him in translation. And at the next dawn, the flag without ceremony before an empty plaza: the conquest needs no witnesses; it needs paperwork.

## ep01-sc10 — THE OATH AND THE BALL (INT. CHAPTER HALL / TERTULIA DRAWING ROOM — NIGHT)

**Fuente** [08:47–09:35]: "el 5 de julio buena parte de los funcionarios del virreinato le juran lealtad al rey británico, a Jorge III. […] para que de un día para el otro pasen a jurar por otro rey… es fuerte. Y además los militares ingleses pasan a vivir en las casas de la alta sociedad de Buenos Aires. Forman parte de las tertulias, de los bailes. […] como si un nuevo orden se hubiese instalado sin ningún tipo de sacudón. —Y los reciben. —Y los reciben."

**Action**: Brief title card: **5 JULY.** The chapter hall: a line of officials swears, one by one, hand upon the Gospels, before Beresford in full dress. One hesitates the space of a sigh — Beresford waits, courteous, motionless — and swears. — Cut to the drawing room of a great house, that night: harpsichord, candles, laughter; British officers dance with criolla young ladies; toasts. The SLAVE of the ensemble serves the glasses with the lowered gaze that registers everything; a MAIDSERVANT gathers cups. Outside, in the mud of the street, the PORTER stops a moment before the lit window; watches the ball; walks on.

**Dialogue**:

**SENIOR OFFICIAL** *(hand on the Gospels, the flat voice of paperwork)*: …I swear fidelity and obedience to His Majesty King George the Third…

*(Cut. The drawing room.)*

**HOSTESS:** Your Excellency honours this house.

**BERESFORD** *(English, a slight bow)*: Madam, the honour is the Crown's.

**YOUNG LADY** *(to the officer she is dancing with, coquettish)*: And in London, Lieutenant — do they dance like this, or do you arrive late there as well?

**OFFICER 1** *(in phrasebook Spanish, delighted with himself)*: In London… there are… no señoritas like this.

*(Laughter. At the far end of the drawing room, by the drinks table:)*

**SLAVE** *(to the maidservant, a whisper, his face unmoving)*: They swore. This morning. All of them.

**MAIDSERVANT:** All of them?

**SLAVE:** The ones who eat indoors.

**Visual note**: The candle chandelier doubled in the tall window; on the other side of the glass, the porter motionless under the drizzle, watching the ball from the mud. Two cities parted by a pane of glass: the one that swears and dances, and the one about to rise. The shot belongs to the one watching from outside.

## ep01-sc11 — COWARD AND TRAITOR (INT. PULPERÍA / EXT. STREETS — DUSK)

**Fuente** [10:23]: "Rápidamente, para la población de Buenos Aires, pasa a ser un cobarde y un traidor; no va a haber demasiadas dudas de esto." [12:45–13:05]: "Bartolomé Mitre dice así: 'Los hombres lloraban de vergüenza por las plazas y las calles.' […] Ignacio Núñez: 'El sentimiento de humillación fue el único que formó todas las conciencias.'"

**Action**: The pulpería at half-light: the PULPERO behind the counter, the PORTER, the FISHERMAN, the FATHER. The news of the flight — confirmed, days later, by the post-riders from the countryside — runs from table to table like a glass tipping over. — Cut to the street, dusk: an OLDER MAN weeps standing, in silence, against a window grille; further on another, sitting on a doorstep, face in his hands. No one mocks. People pass and lower their eyes, because they understand. The FATHER and the BOY see them on their way home.

**Dialogue**:

**PORTER:** A drover came in from the countryside. He saw him. The viceroy, on the road to Córdoba — with the gold.

**PULPERO** *(drops his voice an octave, pours without looking)*: By ox-cart. Nice and slow.

**FISHERMAN:** And us?

**PULPERO:** We stay. Us, and the English.

**FATHER:** Coward.

**PORTER:** Traitor.

*(The two words hang in the air of the pulpería as one day it will stand written: without much room for doubt.)*

— EXT. STREET:

**BOY** *(in a low voice, squeezing his father's hand)*: Papa… why is that man crying?

**FATHER:** From shame, son.

**BOY:** Then why doesn't he fight?

**FATHER** *(a long silence; he looks at his own saddler's hands)*: Because he doesn't yet know what with.

**Visual note**: Mitre's image made shot: grown men weeping on their feet, in silence, through the grey streets of their city. In the pulpería, the counter rag twisting in the pulpero's hands until it is wrung dry — rage that has not yet found its shape.

## ep01-sc12 — WITH STONES (INT. FONDA — NIGHT)

**Fuente** [16:45–17:34]: "En el libro de Gillespie se cuenta esta anécdota — él es el que la trae […] Dice que la muchacha —Marcela— le dice lo siguiente al criollo: 'Desearía que ustedes, caballeros, nos hubiesen informado con anticipación de sus cobardes intenciones de rendir Buenos Aires, pues apostaría mi vida a que, de haberlo sabido las mujeres, nos habríamos levantado unánimemente y rechazado a los ingleses a pedradas.' […] son sintomáticas de un estado más general […] el pulso de lo que va a pasar está mucho más en la boca de una trabajadora anónima que en cualquier otro lado." Eco recreado (no cita) de [15:09]: "lo que valemos las criollas". (Escena de ficción propia — dec-003.)

**Action**: Days into the occupation: the fonda now has a routine of two worlds. GILLESPIE and his TWO OFFICERS dine at their usual table; at another, TWO CRIOLLO merchants, too much wine, too much defeat. MARCELA serves, comes and goes, hears everything. On the counter, the embers of the hearth.

**Dialogue**:

**CRIOLLO 1:** In the end, proclamation or no proclamation, the silver will come in all the same. England pays better than Spain — any man knows that.

**CRIOLLO 2** *(glancing sideways at the uniforms)*: Keep your voice down.

**CRIOLLO 1:** Why? They don't understand. *(raises his glass toward the British table, a loose smile)* To peace, then.

*(Marcela arrives with the plates. Sets them down. Dries her hands on her apron — slowly, her rite. When she speaks, she does not raise her voice: she speaks in a serving tone, plate in hand, in front of the Englishmen who do not understand.)*

**MARCELA:** A pity, gentlemen.

**CRIOLLO 1:** What's a pity, girl?

**MARCELA:** That you gave us no warning, in good time, of your cowardly intentions to hand over Buenos Aires. *(beat)* I'd wager my life: had the women known, we'd have risen all together — and driven the English out with stones.

*(Silence. Criollo 1's glass hangs halfway. At the British table, the officers go on eating — they did not understand. Gillespie did stop. He lowers his fork.)*

**OFFICER 1** *(English, amused, mouth full)*: What did she say?

**GILLESPIE** *(English, his eyes never leaving Marcela)*: That the war hasn't started yet.

*(The officers laugh; Gillespie does not. He wets his pencil. Writes, by candlelight, word for word. Marcela already has her back turned, gathering plates. Passing the hearth, she stops for a second.)*

**MARCELA** *(almost to herself, stirring the embers)*: One day they'll know what we criollas are worth.

*(The embers flare under the iron. On the red of the fire — the only red that is not a soldier's coat — cut to black.)*

**Visual note**: The greatest threat in the film delivered in a serving tone, without setting down the plate. The candle over Gillespie's notebook: the first written line of the Reconquest is dictated by a fonda serving-girl and taken down by the enemy. Last image: Marcela's reddened hands and the embers flaring — the fire that, from here on, never goes out.

**END OF EPISODE 1.**
